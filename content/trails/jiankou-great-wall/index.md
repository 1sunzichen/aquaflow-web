## ⚠️ Important Warning

This is a **wild, unrestored section** of the Great Wall. The access route described here **can be closed at any time** without advance notice — authorities occasionally block entry without warning.

**Last confirmed open: May 10, 2026.** Always verify before you go. If in doubt, [contact us](/contact) and we'll check for you.

---

## Getting There

Navigate to **北沟村委会 (Beigou Village Committee)** using Amap (高德地图) or Baidu Maps. This is your starting point.

**By car:** Drive directly to the village committee. There is space to park nearby.

**By public transit:** Take bus to Huairou, then a local taxi or rideshare to Beigou Village (~¥50–80).

---

## The Route

### Step 1 — Find the Public Toilet

At the village committee, look for the **public toilet** at the front of the area. It's a useful landmark and hard to miss.

### Step 2 — Walk Past the Toilet

Continue walking past the toilet in the same direction. You'll follow a path heading toward the mountain.

### Step 3 — Registration Point

After a short walk, you'll reach a **checkpoint where you must register before going up**. Stop here, fill in your details, and continue. This is mandatory — don't skip it.

### Step 4 — The Black Cow Landmark (~2 km from registration)

Keep walking for roughly **2 km after the registration point**. When you spot a **black cow** on the roadside, you're at the right place. This is your turning point.

### Step 5 — The Spiral Concrete Road

From the black cow, turn and look for the **winding concrete road heading steeply uphill**. Follow it all the way up — it spirals as it climbs. This section is the most physically demanding part of the route.

### Step 6 — The Watchtower

At the top of the concrete road, you'll reach a **watchtower (敌楼)**. From here the views of the unrestored wall stretching across the ridgeline are spectacular. This is the reward.

## What to Expect

```
Village Committee → past toilet → Registration → 2 km walk
→ Black Cow → Spiral concrete road → Watchtower
```

- The trail is steep and has no handrails
- Footing is uneven — hiking boots with grip are essential
- No water sources on the way up — bring at least 2L
- The concrete road looks easy but the gradient is relentless

---

## Difficulty & Fitness

This is a **hard** route. You don't need climbing experience, but you need:
- Good cardio fitness
- Solid ankles (lots of uneven stone and gravel)
- No fear of heights (the wall sections are exposed)

Not recommended for beginners or anyone with knee problems.

---

## What to Bring

- Hiking boots (non-negotiable)
- 2L+ water
- Snacks / energy food
- Trekking poles (helpful on the descent)
- Sun protection
- Fully charged phone — GPS is useful if you lose the path

---

## Best Time to Go

**Early morning on weekdays.** The trail is much less crowded before 9 AM. Avoid weekends in May and October when the village sees more visitors.

Spring (April–May) is ideal: cool temperatures, clear skies, and the surrounding forest is green without being overgrown.

---

## A Note on Access

Jiankou is one of the most visited wild wall sections near Beijing precisely because it hasn't been commercialized. That status is fragile. Local authorities can and do close access at any time — sometimes for safety, sometimes for construction, sometimes without explanation.

**This guide reflects what worked on May 10, 2026.** The path, registration point, and landmarks described here were all in place on that date. Things change. Go soon if you're planning to go.
